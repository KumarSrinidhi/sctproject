# Measurement-technique bias in the ARGUS dataset

_Generated by `argus analyze bias` from `data/processed/measurements_enriched.csv`. Read-only analysis; no record was changed._

## Summary

- **Same-sample anisotropy is large.** 7 samples were reported in both directions (P031, P078, P082, P088): k_in/k_through has median 6.7 (range 0.96–7.7).
- **So the method alone can change k by up to ~2.8×.** An isotropic-mode Hot Disk / hot bridge reads ≈ √(k_in·k_through): 2.6× the laser-flash through-plane value at the median anisotropy (range 1.0–2.8×).
- **No sample in the dataset was measured by two different techniques** (0 same-sample method pairs), so the direct method effect cannot be measured from this literature; only between-paper associations can.
- **Between papers**, isotropic-mode transient sensors report 1.43 (0.86–2.36)× the k of laser flash at the same loading (BN platelets; 14 vs 15 papers): the 95% interval includes 1, so the data are consistent with no effect as well as with the physically expected one. A naive regression that treats rows as independent would give 1.39 (1.10–1.75), overstating certainty.
- **Paper-to-paper scatter dwarfs instrument precision.** After loading, filler and method, papers differ by a factor of 1.88 (1 SD) while instrument uncertainty is ~8% (median judgement default). Sample preparation, filler grade and orientation, not instrument precision, dominate.

The 'method not stated' group falls from 1.66 to 1.37 when abstract-only and figure-digitized rows are excluded (16 of its 51 rows drop out). Abstracts tend to quote a paper's best result, so abstract-only rows are likely biased upward and analyses should weight or exclude them.

## 5. Caveats

- Method effects in section 2 are between-paper associations, not causal estimates: each paper also differs in filler grade, dispersion, curing and sample geometry.
- 'Isotropic-mode sensors' groups TPS, THB and hot wire; whether each paper used an anisotropic module is rarely stated.
- Loading is partly converted from wt% with reference densities (`phi_basis`); k_m is a family default where not reported (`k_matrix_basis`). The `reported_km` fit checks the latter.
- Default uncertainties are judgement values from the research report, not standards.
