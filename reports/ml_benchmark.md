# ML benchmark: predicting k for an unseen paper

_Generated by `argus ml benchmark`. Every number below comes from models that never saw the test paper (except the row-level `random5` column, shown to expose leakage)._

## Summary

- **ensemble predicts k within a typical factor of 1.72** for a paper it has never seen (leave-one-paper-out MAE 0.236 in log10; R² 0.52; 71% of rows within a factor of 2).
- **Acceptance passes:** the best baseline (physics) reaches ×2.06 (MAE 0.315).
- **The ensemble's members were chosen by comparing combinations on these same folds**, so its edge over the best single model (xgboost, MAE 0.012 lower) is not evidence on its own. Its out-of-sample confirmation is the test on other groups' data (reports/external_validation.md).
- **Row-level random splits overstate accuracy.** For knn, random 5-fold MAE is 0.275 vs 0.368 when papers are kept together. Published R² of 0.95–0.99 from random splits are not comparable.
- **Honest uncertainty is large.** 90% paper-grouped intervals cover 90% of rows but span ×/÷3.4 around the prediction: composition alone does not pin down k, because processing and measurement differences between papers dominate (see reports/method_bias.md).

## 1. Data

377 single-filler rows from 119 papers with a known loading; target `log10(k / k_matrix_effective)` (SD 0.4713). 26 features in groups: direction, filler, loading, matrix, method, morphology, physics, quality, treatment. Hybrid-filler rows are excluded (no volume fraction).

## Caveats

- 119 papers is small: differences between learned models of a few 0.01 log10 are within noise (see the ± column).
- Loading is partly converted from wt% and k_m is partly a family default (`*_basis` columns); both add error that no model can remove.
- The time split is harder than leave-one-paper-out. Papers from 2022 on differ from older ones: aligned fillers 36% vs 16% of rows and a lower median enhancement (0.66 vs 0.78 log10). A model trained on older papers meets a shifted population. On this split the best model is gp_residual (MAE 0.290).
- Intervals are calibrated on average over papers, not for every paper.
