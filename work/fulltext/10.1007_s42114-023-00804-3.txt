Abstract
With the continuous innovation of electronic information technology, thermal interface materials, which
mainly play the role of heat dissipation in microelectronic devices, will face great challenges. In this work,
the boron nitride whiskers (BNWK)@Al2O3/cellulose aerogels (CA) obtained by electrostatic self-assembly
one-dimensional BNWK and zero-dimensional nano-Al2O3 combined with directional freezing of CA. The
obtained BNWK@Al2O3/CA not only has a unique vertical network structure, but also exhibits exceptional
compressive mechanical strength, especially when the mass ratio of BNWK/nano-Al2O3 is 1:7, the
compressive strength of BNWK@Al2O3(1:7)/CA reaches 97 kPa, based on the flexibility of the CA and the
support of the rigid hybrid filler BNWK@Al2O3, the theoretical relaxation time of the composite is also as
high as 25327 s. Furthermore, the thermal conductivity of the epoxy-based composite
(BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP) with a filler loading of 8.6 wt% is about 1.92 W/(m·K), which is 9.6 times that of
pure EP, the excellent thermally conductive property is due to the accelerated phonon transport by the
vertically arranged BNWK@Al2O3 network structure. Hence, this work provides a new idea for developing
a new generation of thermal interface materials.
1 Introduction
With the development of science and technology, the power and performance of various electronic
devices have been greatly improved [1–3]. It follows that the heat dissipation problem inside the
electronic components will face a major test [4, 5]. Polymers are widely used in microelectronic packaging
materials due to their excellent physical and chemical properties, however, their intrinsic thermal
conductivity is generally low, and they can no longer meet the needs of practical production as heat
dissipation interface materials alone [6, 7].
At present, filler-filled polymer-based composites are an effective strategy to solve the problem of efficient
heat dissipation of interface materials [8, 9]. These fillers mainly include metal and metal oxide particles
(such as Ag, Zn, Cu and their corresponding oxides, etc.) [5, 10, 11], carbon-based materials (carbon black,
graphite and carbon nanotubes, etc.) [12, 13] and ceramic materials (aluminum oxide, silicon carbide and
boron nitride, etc.) [14, 15]. Among them, metal and metal oxide particles as well as carbon-based
materials have high electrical conductivity, which violates the basic insulating properties that electronic
packaging materials need to meet in practical applications [16]. While ceramic materials are considered
to be the most promising thermally conductive fillers due to their good electrical insulation and excellent
chemical stability [17]. In particular, boron nitride (BN), known as white graphite, has become a star
thermally conductive filler because of its excellent properties such as high thermal conductivity, low
dielectric loss and corrosion resistance [18, 19]. For instance, Pan et al. [20] obtained a highly thermally
conductive epoxy-based composite (2.11 W/(m·K)) which possesses a unique three-dimensional (3D)
carbonized cellulose/BN aerogel network structure. As a typical one-dimensional boron nitride single
crystal material, boron nitride whiskers (BNWK) have gradually become a hot material due to their highly
ordered atomic arrangement, low crystal defects and outstanding advantages in directional heat transfer

[21]. In addition, aluminum oxide (Al2O3), as another ceramic thermal conductive filler, is also widely used
in the synthesis of thermal management composites based on its low price [22]. Bian et al. [23] chosen
modified nano-Al2O3 and BN as fillers for fabricating epoxy-based composites, and found that the
thermal conductivity of the composite was 700% higher than that of pure epoxy.
For the preparation of thermal management composites, traditional blending methods are limited by the
low promotion efficiency of thermal conductivity and generally require a larger filler content, while large
loadings are bound to degrade mechanical and other properties of the composite [24, 25]. Therefore, there
is a great need for new processing strategies to prepare excellent thermally conductive composites with
low filler contents. Based on isotropic composites with high thermal conductivity required to build
complex and perfect thermally conductive filler network structures which could further excavate the
potential of polymer-based composites [26], in recent years, more and more researchers began to focus
on the orientation distribution of fillers in the polymer to achieve a rapid heat dissipation of the
composites in a certain direction [27]. At present, the methods included in this strategy contain magnetic
field or electric field orientation method, electrospinning method and vacuum filtration method, etc [28–
30]. Although there have been many studies on improving the overall thermal conductivity of composites
by constructing directional thermal pathway strategies, the above-mentioned methods have high
technical challenge.
In this work, one-dimensional BNWK and nano-Al2O3 are used as composite fillers for the first time to
obtain a BNWK/Al2O3/cellulose aerogel (CA) vertically oriented framework with certain mechanical
strength through simple electrostatic self-assembly and directional freeze-drying. Finally, it was found
that the thermal conductivity of the epoxy-based composite BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP obtained by vacuum
impregnation EP was as high as 1.92 W/(m·K) when the mass ratio of BNWK and Nano-Al2O3 was 1:7
and the total content was only 8.6 wt%. The excellent thermally conductive performance is mainly
attributed to the stable vertically oriented framework and efficient phonon transport pathway.
2 Experimental
2.1 Materials
BN whiskers (BNWK, diameters: 0.4–1.0 µm, length: 5–10 µm) were purchased from Beijing Dake Daojin
Science and Technology Co., Ltd., China. Nano-Al2O3 (γ phase, ~ 20 nm, 99%), ammonium hydroxide
solution (NH4OH, 25–28%), N,N′-Methylenebis(acrylamide) (MBA, C7H10N2O2, 1.235 g/cm3), Sodium
hydroxide (NaOH, AR, 96%) and Urea (H2NCONH2, AR, 99%) were supplied by Shanghai McLean
Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., China. Cellulose (length: ≤ 25 mm) and ethanol (CH3CH2OH, AR, 95%)
were provided by Shanghai Aladdin Reagent Co., Ltd., China. Epoxy (EP, E-44) and amine curing agent 593
were obtained from WuXi Pinhua Chemical Co., Ltd., China. Deionized (DI) water was supplied in
unlimited quantities by the laboratory.

2.2 Preparation of BNWK@Al2O3 hybrid filler
The preparation of BNWK@Al2O3 hybrid fillers is based on a simple and environmentally friendly water-
phase electrostatic self-assembly strategy. The detailed preparation process is shown in Fig. S1, first, 1 g
BNWK was diluted into 5 mg/mL dispersion with DI water, and the solution pH value was adjusted to
about 11 with aqueous ammonia to obtain a dispersion with a negative Zeta potential. Next, a series of
mass (1 g, 3 g, 5 g and 7 g) of nano-Al2O3 were weighed and dissolved in 200 mL of a mixed solution of
ethanol and DI water (Vethanol: VDI = 3:1) to obtain a dispersion with a positive Zeta potential. Finally, the
above two dispersions were fully mixed under mechanical stirring for 3 h, followed by centrifugation and
drying to obtain BNWK@Al2O3 hybrid filler powder. The obtained series of hybrid fillers are labeled as
BNWK@Al2O3 (1:1), BNWK@Al2O3 (1:3), B BNWK@Al2O3 (1:5) and BNWK@Al2O3 (1:7), respectively.
2.3 Preparation of BNWK@Al2O3/cellulose aerogel
(BNWK@Al2O3/CA)
The BNWK@Al2O3/cellulose aerogel (BNWK@Al2O3/CA) was obtained from the corresponding
BNWK@Al2O3/cellulose hydrogel (Fig. S1), and the specific preparation process of aerogel is similar to
our previous research works [20, 31]. To simplify writing, the obtained BNWK@Al2O3/cellulose hydrogel
and BNWK@Al2O3/cellulose aerogel were marked as BNWK@Al2O3/CH and BNWK@Al2O3/CA,
respectively.
2.4 Preparation of BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP composites
The BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP composites were obtained by a vacuum-assisted impregnation strategy, the
specific operation can learn from our previous research work [32, 33]. In addition, BNWK/CA/EP with the
same synthesis method, and for comparison, BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP(Blend) composites were also
prepared by a simple blending.
2.5 Characterization
The microstructure and morphology of the synthesized materials were observed through scanning
electron microscope (SEM; Thermo Scientific Apreo 2). The Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy
(FTIR, Nicolet 6700) with a test range of 500–4000 cm− 1. Zeta potentials of samples were investigated
by a Zeta potential analyzer (Zetasizernano). The thermal gravimetric analyzer (TGA 2) was used to
accurately determine filler content. The thermal conductivity was measured by a Hot Disk Thermal
Constant Analyzer (TPS 2500S, Sweden) and the infrared radiation intensity over time of the composites
were measured by an infrared thermal camera (E60, FLIR). The compression test by using UTM2203
microcomputer with a compression rate of 5 mm/s.

3.1 Characterization
The zeta potentials of BNWK dispersion and Al2O3 dispersion are demonstrated in Fig. 1a. Due to the
coating of hydroxide ions in an alkaline environment, BNWK exhibits a negative zeta potential of − 49.7
mV. Meanwhile, the Al2O3 dispersion presents positive zeta potential (26.5 mV). The relatively large
charge of BNWK (|-49.7|>|26.5|) facilitates the deposition of more nano-Al2O3 on its surface via
electrostatic interactions. From Fig. 1b, a single BN exhibits the characteristic peaks at ~ 795 cm− 1 for the
B–N–B bending vibrations and at ~ 1375 cm− 1 for the B–N stretching vibrations [34]. The peaks at 818
and 552 cm− 1 are the infrared absorption peaks of nano-Al2O3 [35]. After electrostatic self-assembly, the
infrared spectra of the hybrid fillers contain all the above characteristic peaks, wherein the characteristic
peak intensity of nano-Al2O3 increases with the increase of its content (From BNWK@Al2O3 (1:1) to
BNWK@Al2O3 (1:7)). Furthermore, the peak at ~ 3475 cm− 1 can be found in all samples due to the
presence of adsorbed water on the filler surface.
The microscopic morphologies of nano-Al2O3, BNWK, and hybrid filler BNWK@Al2O3 with different quality
ratios are shown in Fig. 2. From Fig. 2a, due to size effect, nano-Al2O3 aggregates to form heterogeneous
clusters. It can be seen from the Fig. 2b that BNWK presents smooth rod-like morphology with a diameter
of 0.5–1.5 µm and a length of 5–10 µm. Figure 2c − f show the SEM images of BNWK@Al2O3 with
different mass ratios of BNWK/Al2O3 are1:1, 1:3, 1:5 and 1:7, respectively. It can be clearly seen that
nano-Al2O3 uniformly loads on the surface of BNWK by electrostatic action, and with the increase of
nano-Al2O3 content, its loading on the surface of BNWK gradually increases until it completely covers
BNWK at the mass ratio of BNWK to nano-Al2O3 is 1:7.
In order to demonstrate the vertically oriented structure of fillers in cellulose aerogels, the side-view
images of different samples were studied. During directional-freezing, the water in the hydrogel gradually
turns into ice crystals and transitions from the bottom to the top [36]. As shown in Fig. 3a, pure CAs
obtained by directional freezing exhibit continuous oriented pore structures [37, 38], and the light-weight
property of CAs are reflected from the illustration [39, 40]. While the addition of BNWK and hybrid filler
BNWK@Al2O3 did not affect the vertical skeleton structure formed by ice crystals (Fig. 3b-f). From Fig. 3b,
BNWKs (marked in the red circles) are oriented along the CA wall under the extrusion of ice crystals, for
which the heat conduction pathways are marked by orange lines. Obviously, with the introduction of the
hybrid filler BNWK@Al2O3 and the increase of nano-Al2O3 content, the thermal conduction pathway
framework in the CA becomes thicker, (Fig. 3c-f), which provides an effective theoretical support for the
ideal mechanical support of composite. Especially, when the mass ratio of BNWK to nano-Al2O3 is 1:7,
the nano-Al2O3 bound on the surface of BNWK through electrostatic self-assembly begins to connect with
each other between adjacent vertical skeleton BNWKs, which is greatly beneficial to the heat dissipation
performance [41].
3.2 Mechanical properties

Page 7/23
The compressive mechanical properties of the aerogel are critical to determining its durability and
suitability for thermal conduction applications [42, 43]. The size of the test samples is 20 mm in diameter
and 10 mm in height, and the compressive stress-strain curves of different CAs are shown in Fig. 4a. All
aerogels can be compressed to about 50% strain and exhibit excellent flexibility. Pure CA exhibits a near-
linear compressive stress response up to 15.2 kPa, and there is an obvious fracture in the later period.
When BNWK or different contents of hybrid fillers (BNWK@Al2O3) were introduced into CA, the stress-
strain curves of the composites exhibit three stages of foam-like structural characteristics, including
linear elastic region at low strain values (less than 25%), plastic region at medium strain values (from
25–40%) and densification stage at high strain values (over 40%). It is clear to see that the
BNWK@Al2O3/CA with different ratios (1:1, 1:3, 1:5 and 1:7) of BNWK/Al2O3 have a compressive stress of
33, 46, 69, and 97 kPa at 50% strain, respectively, which is much higher than that of 24 kPa for the
BNWK/CA.

composite, the stress relaxation of different aerogel samples under 50% constant strain was analyzed in

In practical application, the interfacial thermal resistance between filler and matrix in thermal

Figure 5d shows the comparison of the thermally conductive effect between our composite and other
representative BN filled composites reported in the literature [55–60], among them, the filler content of
this work is obtained by thermogravimetric analysis test (Fig. S3). Obviously, our work exhibits a high
thermal conductivity than those of other composites at the lower filler contents.
To confirm the actual thermally conductive performances of the samples, an infrared thermal imager was
used to record their temperature change with time [61, 62]. Specifically, for the heating test, as shown in
Fig. 6(a2), the as-prepared samples were vertically placed on a heating plate (set to 80°C) at the same
time, and the average surface temperatures of the different samples were measured at an interval of
every 20 s to obtain infrared thermal images (Fig. 6(a1)) and the data of surface temperature (Fig. 6(a3)).
As expected, the surface temperature of different of composites gradually increases, After 80 s, the
surface temperature of BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP is up to about 74.8°C, which is much than the pure EP
(62.7°C), BNWK/CA/EP (67.3°C) and BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP (Blend) with ordinary blending method
(68.9°C).
While for the cooling process, the heated samples were quickly transferred to a foam plate and the
change in surface temperature was recorded (Fig. 6(b1) and (b2)). Similar to the heating process, the
BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP still shows excellent heat dissipation capacity. It can quickly drop from 77.2°C to
about 30.5°C within 120 s (Fig. 6(b3)). The above results demonstrate that the BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP has
an extremely high heat transfer efficiency and will be expected to be deployed in the heat dissipation for
the micro-electronic devices.
3.4 Thermally conductive mechanism of different
composites
Based on the above morphological characterization and thermal performance test results, the thermally
conductive mechanism of the four systems of epoxy resin-based composites is presented in Fig. 7a. For
pure EP, the heat flux is difficult to transmit in the vertical direction due to the disordered arrangement of

Page 10/23
its molecular chains and the severe scattering of phonons [63]. The thermal conductivity of BNWK/CA/EP
with vertically aligned BNWK increases from 0.2 to 0.48 W/(m·K) compared to pure EP. However, due to
the limited heat conduction pathway formed by vertical BNWK and the inability to form an efficient
phonon transmission path, the thermal conductivity did not increase significantly. For
BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP (Blend) with randomly dispersed hybrid fillers (BNWK@Al2O3) by ordinary blending
method, although a thermally conductive network structure is formed inside, the weak interaction
between the hybrid fillers and the matrix EP hinders the further improvement of thermal conductivity.
Combined with the advantages of internal filler distribution of BNWK/CA/EP and BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP
(Blend), the thermal conductivity of BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP system with vertically arranged hybrid filler
BNWK@Al2O3 has been significantly improved in the vertical direction. The reason is that on one hand,
BNWK forms fast phonon transmission paths in the vertical direction, and on the other hand, the
electrostatically assembled nano-Al2O3 on the surface of BNWK is connected to each other to form an
effective thermal conduction network.
In order to clearly see the thermal conduction process of the sample from the macroscopic level, we
adopted the finite element analysis (COMSOL Multiphysics) technique [64, 65]. The specific modeling and
analysis process is presented in the Supporting Information. Figure 7b shows the steady-state
temperature distribution images of the models with different composites. From the simulation results, we
can clearly see that the heat flow of BNWK@Al2O3/CA/EP can rapidly cover the entire sample from the
bottom in the same time due to its unique vertical structure and thermal conductive network structure
[66].
4 Conclusions
In summary, the hybrid filler BNWK@Al2O3 was first synthesized by an electrostatic self-assembly
method, and then a unique vertical network structure (BNWK@Al2O3/CA) was successfully constructed by
introducing BNWK@Al2O3 into cellulose aerogel (CA) with the help of directional freezing technology of
cellulose hydrogel. The obtained BNWK@Al2O3/CA not only exhibits exceptional compressive mechanical
strength, especially when the mass ratio of BNWK and nano-Al2O3 is 1:7, the compressive strength of
BNWK@Al2O3(1:7)/CA reaches 97 kPa, but also shows a large compressive relaxation time as high as
25327 s based on the flexibility of the cellulose aerogel and the support of the rigid hybrid filler
BNWK@Al2O3. More importantly, the resulting EP-based BNWK@Al2O3(1:7)/CA/EP composites exhibit
excellent thermal management properties in the vertical direction, at a BNWK@Al2O3 loading of 8.6 wt%,
the composites showed a dramatically enhanced thermal conductivity to 1.92 W/mK, which far exceeds
of BNWK@Al2O3(1:7)/CA/EP (Blend) composites obtained by ordinary blending method (0.87 W/mK)
BNWK/CA/EP composites without nano-Al2O3 (0.48 W/mK), and pure EP (0.2 W/mK). Besides, the finite
element heat conduction simulation further analyzes the heat conduction mechanism from the
theoretical level. Therefore, this work provides a new idea for the development of a new generation of
thermal interface materials.
