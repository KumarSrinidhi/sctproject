# Thermal-conductivity measurement techniques for polymer–ceramic (h-BN / BNNS / AlN – epoxy) composites: principles, standards, direction sensitivity, uncertainty and method-dependent bias

*Scope: room-temperature measurement of solid polymer composites (discs, films, pads, TIMs). Research notes compiled 2026-10-22. Each statement is tagged "vendor" when it comes from an instrument maker or seller and "peer-reviewed" / "standard" where applicable. Numbers I derived myself are listed only under Inferences.*

---

## Q1. Technique-by-technique: principle, equation, standard, measured quantity, direction sensed, uncertainty, sample needs, pitfalls (including Chinese instruments)

### Takeaway
Bulk polymer composites are measured mainly by four families. (1) Flash methods (LFA/xenon flash) measure diffusivity through the thickness, and k = α·ρ·Cp. (2) Plane- or line-source transient probes (Hot Disk TPS, Linseis THB, hot wire / QTM, C-Therm MTPS) report a k that is direction-averaged on anisotropic samples. (3) Steady-state 1-D heat-flow rigs (ASTM D5470, E1530 guarded heat-flow meter, C177/C518 plates, Chinese "DRL" testers) measure through-plane thermal resistance, contact resistance included. (4) Thin-film or microscale methods (3ω, TDTR/FDTR, lock-in IR thermography, photothermal radiometry) are seldom used for filled-epoxy discs. In the dataset's strings, "DRL-III" is a steady-state D5470-type heat-flow tester, "quick thermal conductivity meter" is almost certainly a KEM QTM hot-wire probe, and "TC 3000" is a Xi'an XIATECH transient hot-wire instrument.

### Cited Findings

**General / cross-cutting**
- A review of bulk methods names the four most used: steady-state, transient hot-wire, laser flash and TPS. For thin films it names 3ω and TDTR/FDTR. It states that "it is a very challenging task to determine thermal conductivity and interfacial thermal conductance with less than 5% error" (peer-reviewed; Zhao, Qian, Gu, Jajja, Yang, *J. Electron. Packag.* 2016) — [Zhao et al. arXiv 1605.08469](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469)

**A. Laser / xenon flash analysis (LFA)**
- Principle (Parker et al.): a pulse heats the front face and an IR detector records the rear-face rise. The method assumes 1-D conduction with no lateral loss. Graphite is sprayed on both faces to act as absorber and emitter. The half-rise solution is α = 1.38·L²/(π²·t½), and the full series is T(t)/Tmax = 1 + 2Σ(−1)ⁿexp(−n²π²αt/L²) — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469)
- Standards: ASTM E1461 and ISO 22007-4. The review reports uncertainty "less than 3%" (for diffusivity), sample diameters of about 5–12 mm, a minimum thickness of about 100 µm on commercial instruments (set by pulse and detector time-scales), and 1–2 s per shot. Density and Cp must come from separate experiments, which leads to a "stack up" of uncertainties. Heating of the sample holder can cause significant error — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469)
- ASTM E1461 scope covers "primarily homogeneous isotropic solid materials", 0.1–1000 mm²/s, about 75–2800 K (E1461-13, reapproved 2022) — [normservis listing of ASTM E1461-13(2022)](https://eshop.normservis.sk/norma/astm-e1461-13_2022-1.4.2022.html)
- Direction: the standard geometry heats one face and detects the opposite face, so it measures **through-plane** diffusivity. ISO 22007-4 / ASTM E1461 LFA on disc samples is described as a through-plane measurement — [search summary of BN-composite literature, e.g. PMC10347135](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10347135/)
- **In-plane LFA:** a first mask shapes the flash into a ring ("doughnut") and a second mask limits the detector view to the centre spot, which forces radial diffusion (TA DLF 1200 in-plane fixture, tested on a 35 µm graphite sheet) — [TA Instruments note](https://tainstruments.com/?p=186047). NETZSCH describes the same ring-illumination / centre-detection approach and a two-mask PEEK holder — [NETZSCH anisotropic LFA note](https://analyzing-testing.netzsch.com/en/application-literature/laser-flash-analysis-makes-heat-transfer-in-anisotropic-materials-measurable); [NETZSCH AN454](https://analyzing-testing.netzsch.com/_Resources/Persistent/e/2/4/0/e240feb3d27e4e801772997dd0b2dfa6471bc492/AN454_~1.pdf). In-plane α of free-standing h-BN laminates was measured on a NETZSCH LFA 457 with a special holder (about 22 mm membrane, 5 mm heated spot, graphite paint on sample and holder) — [hBN laminate paper, arXiv 1512.04586](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.04586)
- An alternative in-plane route uses only the standard through-plane holder: stack and glue the discs, then cut a 2 × 10 × 10 mm³ slice parallel to the through-plane direction and run ISO 22007-4 on it. This is the procedure in a polymer/BN compound patent — [US10328620B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10328620B2/en)
- NETZSCH LFA 467 HyperFlash spec (vendor): accuracy ±3% for diffusivity and ±5% for Cp; repeatability ±2% for diffusivity and ±3% for Cp — [NETZSCH LFA 467 technical specifications](https://analyzing-testing.netzsch.com/_Resources/Persistent/f/9/0/2/f902a7aebecc31191c0f0d9d2ba6d9cb9f9d0e7b/Technical_Specifications_en_LFA_467_Hyperflash_LFA_467_HT_Hyperflash.pdf)
- Thin polymer films (vendor): the LFA 467 with a thin-sample holder can measure films of about 20 µm. NETZSCH recommends sputtered gold for opacity and says graphite "is not recommended for such thin samples since it might influence the measurement results" — [NETZSCH thin polymer film note](https://analyzing-testing.netzsch.com/pl/application-literature/thermal-diffusivity-of-extremely-thin-polymer-films)

**B. Transient plane source (TPS, Hot Disk) — ISO 22007-2, ASTM E3088**
- Principle: a nickel double-spiral sensor sealed in polyimide is both heater and resistance thermometer. It is clamped between two identical flat samples, and the temperature rise is fitted as ΔT(τ) ∝ P₀/(π^1.5·r·k)·D(τ), with τ = √(α t)/r. Error sources are sensor–sample contact resistance, sensor thermal inertia, the heat capacity of the insulating films and changes in sensor resistance. The method needs one fully planar face on each sample piece — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469)
- Hot Disk (vendor): the **isotropic (standard) module** "deduced the geometrical average of the in-plane and through-plane Thermal Conductivity" together with the in-plane diffusivity of the probed volume. Best accuracy comes when the probing depth lies between the sensor radius and diameter. Double-sided testing is the most accurate. Samples can be several cm down to sub-mm thick with the smallest sensors. Standard: ISO 22007-2 (since 2008) and ASTM E3088-25. Range 0.03–500 W/m·K (TPS 2500 S/3500) — [Hot Disk isotropic module](https://www.hotdiskinstruments.com/products/measurement-modules/isotropic-standard-module/)
- Hot Disk **anisotropic module** (vendor): for uniaxial samples, with the sensor perpendicular to the main axis. One transient gives √(k_in·k_through) and the in-plane α. A known (volumetric) specific heat is then used to separate k_in and k_through. Double-sided testing is more accurate — [Hot Disk anisotropic module](https://www.hotdiskinstruments.com/products/measurement-modules/anisotropic-module/)
- A peer-reviewed depth-profiling paper confirms that an isotropic analysis "renders the geometric average thermal conductivity in the radial and normal directions" — [Rev. Sci. Instrum. 94, 074902 (2023)](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/rsi/article/94/7/074902/2901368/Thermal-conductivity-vs-depth-profiling-using-the)
- ISO 22007-2: the 2008 edition was withdrawn. The current edition is ISO 22007-2:2022 — [AFNOR ISO 22007-2:2022](https://www.boutique.afnor.org/en-gb/standard/iso-2200722022/plastics-determination-of-thermal-conductivity-and-thermal-diffusivity-part/xs139162/326465). ISO 22007-7:2023 covers effusivity by a transient plane heat source — [iTeh ISO 22007-7](https://iteh.es/catalog/standards/iso/0dcbee0d-e207-4e02-9955-3288331c6c7a/iso-22007-7-2023)
- TPS 2500 S spec as listed by a user facility: "Reproducibility typically better than 1%, accuracy better than 5%", 0.005–1200 W/m·K — [UAH Thermal Constants Analyzer sheet](https://www.uah.edu/images/research/ri/rfal/Thermal%20Constants%20Analyzer.pdf). Another facility lists ≤±3% for k and ≤±5% for α — [HKUST-GZ listing](https://mcpf.hkust-gz.edu.cn/?p=1003). The two listings disagree.
- Thin films: a Chalmers/Hot Disk paper says direct TPS on thin films "fails due to thermal contact resistance". A multi-thickness method showed errors up to 26% on polyolefin films, reduced to about 4% with a liquid interface — [Chalmers paper](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/547882/file/547882_Fulltext.pdf)
- Low-k samples: raw values from a pristine sensor overestimated k by about 40% (aerogels). Corrections brought the error below 4% (LBNL) — [arXiv 1910.01798](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.01798)
- A study of additively manufactured polymer composites says TPS assumes equal conductivity in two directions, which FFF parts do not have — [ResearchGate 358330428](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358330428_An_accurate_steady-state_approach_for_characterizing_the_thermal_conductivity_of_Additively_manufactured_polymer_composites)

**C. Transient hot bridge (THB, Linseis)**
- Developed with PTB as an "optimized hot-wire technique" for k, α and volumetric heat capacity. The sensor is nickel foil between polyimide sheets, with eight strips switched as an equal-resistance Wheatstone bridge. The bridge goes out of balance under heating, and that voltage rise is analysed. Linseis calls it an absolute method that needs no calibration and compensates for end effects (vendor) — [Linseis THB method page](https://www.linseis.com/en/methods/transient-hot-bridge-thb)
- Brochure specs (vendor): k precision better than 1%, α and volumetric heat capacity better than 4%. Accuracy is better than 3% for most samples with the THB sensor, about 5–10% for the "hot point" sensor (small or anisotropic samples) and below 5% for the QSS sensor. Smallest sample 3×3×2 mm (hot point). Standards cited: ASTM D5930, ISO 22007-2, ASTM D5334 — [Linseis THB brochure](https://linseis.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/THB-Product-brochure.pdf)

**D. Transient hot wire / line-source / needle probe**
- Principle: a line source of power q per unit length gives ΔT = (q/4πk)·ln(t₂/t₁), so k = q·ln(t₂/t₁)/(4π·ΔT). This is the late-time solution of ΔT = (q/4πk)[ln(4αt/r²) − γ]. The method assumes an infinite, isotropic, homogeneous medium. A carefully applied method can reach below 1% uncertainty for gases, liquids and solids, but few commercial instruments exist because the wires are fragile. The needle-probe variant is standardised as ASTM D5930 (plastics) and D5334 (soils). Hot wire for refractories is standardised as ASTM C1113 and ISO 8894 — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469)
- **ASTM D7896 is a fluids standard.** It covers k, α and volumetric heat capacity of engine coolants and related fluids, in the laboratory, with about 0.8 s measurements — [ASTM D7896-19](https://store.astm.org/d7896-19.html). The 2019 version applies to about 0.1–1.0 W/m·K — [normdocs D7896-19](https://catalogue.normdocs.ru/catalog/com.normdocs.astm.card.d7896-19/Standard-ASTM-D7896-19). D7896-26 is the current version — [ASTM D7896-26](https://store.astm.org/d7896-26.html). A 2024 review of commercial transient hot-wire devices says they follow ASTM D7896-19 — [Ricciu et al. 2024, J. Phys. Conf. Ser. 2685](https://airus.unisalento.it/retrieve/d8fea3d2-7362-4da8-b7f6-6b0fd3e0694e/Ricciu_2024_J._Phys.__Conf._Ser._2685_012053.pdf)

**E. Modified transient plane source (MTPS, C-Therm TCi / Trident)**
- One-sided interfacial heat-reflectance sensor with a constant heat pulse of about 1–3 s. The spec table gives accuracy better than 5% and precision better than 1% (vendor; Trident table) — [C-Therm spec PDF hosted by NIT Trichy](https://www.nitt.edu/home/rc/sif/facilities/C%20Therm.pdf). A competitor's listing gives 5% accuracy and 2% repeatability (vendor) — [Thermtest MTPS listing](https://thermtest.com/testing-services/thermal-effusivity)
- **ASTM D7984 covers thermal effusivity of fabrics** (woven, knitted, non-woven) by guarded MTPS. It applies only when the specimen is thicker than the heat-flux penetration depth, is calibrated against known-effusivity specimens and names C-Therm as sole source — [GlobalSpec D7984](https://standards.globalspec.com/std/14462589/d7984). A revision (WK93640) began on 2025-01-28 — [ASTM WK93640](https://store.astm.org/workitem-wk93640). (Zhao et al. cite D7984 as a TPS standard, which is loose — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469).)
- A peer-reviewed MTPS error analysis finds that uncertainty in the thickness of the contact region limits accuracy under conventional single-parameter analysis — [Illinois Experts, RSI 2024](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/analysis-of-heat-flow-in-modified-transient-plane-source-mtps-mea/)

**F. Steady-state methods (guarded hot plate, heat-flow meter, guarded heat-flow meter, D5470)**
- Absolute method: k = Q·Δx/(A·ΔT), with Q = P − Q_loss. Parasitic losses should be kept below 2%. The guarded hot plate is the reference apparatus (ASTM C177, EN 12667, ISO 8302). It needs cm-scale samples and hours to reach steady state. The comparative cut-bar method (ASTM E1225) uses k₁ = k₂(ΔT₂/ΔT₁)(Δx₁/Δx₂) and is most accurate when k_sample ≈ k_reference. Heat-flow-meter methods (ASTM C518, ASTM E1530, EN 12667) use a calibrated heat-flux transducer — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469)
- ASTM E1530 (guarded heat-flow meter): steady-state, for specimens thinner than 25 mm and thermal resistances of 10–400 ×10⁻⁴ m²K/W, which corresponds to roughly 0.1–30 W/m·K at 150–600 K. It can reach up to 60 W/m·K with reduced accuracy — [ASTM E1530-11](https://store.astm.org/e1530-11.html). The 2019 edition calls it "particularly useful for homogeneous, multilayer, and composite specimens" — [E1530-19 listing](https://eshop.normservis.sk/norma/astm-e1530-19-1.2.2019.html). The 1999 edition covered about 0.1–5 W/m·K — [E1530-99 listing](https://eshop.normservis.sk/norma/astm-e1530-99-10.3.1999.html)
- ASTM D5470 ("Thermal Transmission Properties of Thermally Conductive Electrical Insulation Materials"). The current version is D5470-17(2024) — [BSI Knowledge listing](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/standard-test-method-for-thermal-transmission-properties-of-thermally-conductive-electrical-insulation-materials-1). The TIM is clamped between two metered (usually copper) bars, as in Fig. 3c of Zhao et al. — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469). Specific thermal resistance = ΔT_interface·A/Q. The method needs four aligned surfaces, and "misalignments as low as 1% often go undetected" — [Electronics Cooling 2014](https://www.electronics-cooling.com/2014/02/towards-reproducible-astm-d5470-measurements-lower-cost/). D5470 "usually gives poor results" above about 100 W/m·K — [Bulinski et al. 2019](https://yadda.icm.edu.pl/baztech/element/bwmeta1.element.baztech-2e011220-2ccb-4255-b3c6-2630491276ae/c/Bulinski_jamme_2019_95_2.pdf)
- Bulk k and contact resistance can only be separated by testing a series of bond-line thicknesses while pressure, surface finish, cure and temperature are held constant (vendor/blog) — [ACS Material blog](https://www.acsmaterial.com/blog-detail/thermal-interface-material-measurement.html)

**G. Thin-film / microscale methods (3ω, TDTR, FDTR)**
- 3ω: a metal line heater is driven at ω, and the third-harmonic voltage, about 10³ times smaller than the 1ω signal, is read with a lock-in. The substrate's k comes from the slope of the in-phase ΔT against ln(2ω). A wide heater gives cross-plane k. A narrow heater, with half-width b satisfying (k_⊥/k_∥)^½·b/d ≤ 0.1, gives sensitivity to in-plane k. Radiation error is below 2% even at 1000 K. The method needs microfabrication, plus an insulating layer on conducting samples — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469)
- TDTR/FDTR: pump–probe thermoreflectance on a metal transducer (e.g. 100 nm Al) is fitted for k, G and C. At low modulation frequency with a small spot the signal depends on the **geometric average** √(k_r·k_z). At high frequency with a large spot it depends on √(k_z·C), the cross-plane term. Fits are iterated until the change is below tolerance (e.g. 1%). FDTR can run on CW lasers — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469). Asymmetric-beam TDTR is used for anisotropic samples — [AB-TDTR, arXiv 1809.10543](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.10543)
- A review of h-BN/polymer nanocomposites reports that LFA and TDTR were considered insufficient to measure a single 7 µm BN sphere — [Recent advances in h-BN/polymer nanocomposites with isotropic k (ScienceDirect 2024)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949944524000066)

**H. IR / lock-in thermography and photothermal methods**
- Lock-in thermography "slopes method" for the in-plane diffusivity of semitransparent polymer films. It includes convective and radiative losses and IR semitransparency (Philipp et al., *Anal. Chem.* 2019, doi:10.1021/acs.analchem.9b01583) — [EPub Bayreuth](https://epub.uni-bayreuth.de/6422/)
- Lock-in periodic-heating thermography resolved in-plane diffusivity anisotropy in oriented cellulose-nanofibre films. The anisotropy correlated with the WAXS order parameter (*Int. J. Thermophys.* 2022) — [Springer s10765-022-03009-w](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10765-022-03009-w)
- A laser-based Ångström method with IR imaging gives isotropic and anisotropic in-plane properties of films and sheets without needing precise boundary conditions (Gaitonde, Candadai, Weibel, Marconnet) — [CiteDrive record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/a-laser-based-%C3%A5ngstrom-method-for-in-plane-thermal-characterization-of-isotropic-and-anisotropic-materials-using-infrared-imaging). Lock-in micro-thermography for full in-plane tensors (preprint, not peer-reviewed) — [arXiv 2404.11424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.11424v1)
- Modulated photothermal radiometry (PTR) is used for bulk samples and coatings — [arXiv 2008.13086](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.13086)
- ISO 22007-3:2008 (temperature-wave analysis) measures through-thickness diffusivity of thin films and plates, 1×10⁻⁸ to 1×10⁻⁴ m²/s, for solid or molten plastics — [AFNOR ISO 22007-3](https://www.boutique.afnor.org/en-gb/standard/iso-2200732008/plastics-determination-of-thermal-conductivity-and-thermal-diffusivity-part/xs021733/115566)

- **Per-method direction metadata** (to fill `k_direction` where it is "unknown"): LFA standard holder → `through-plane`. D5470 / E1530 / HFM / GHP / DRL → `through-plane` (apparent, contact resistance included unless multi-thickness). TPS isotropic and THB → `mixed_geometric` (≈√(k_in·k_through) with the sensor in the sample plane; this follows from the Hot Disk/RSI statements above for TPS, and I am assuming THB's planar foil sensor behaves the same). Hot wire / QTM probe lying on or embedded in a plate → `mixed` (radial heat flow perpendicular to the wire, so k_eff ≈ √(k_in⊥wire·k_through); this comes from standard line-source theory in anisotropic media and is not sourced above). MTPS → `mixed_unknown_weighting`. TDTR at high frequency / large spot → `cross-plane_local`. Recommendation: add a fourth `k_direction` value, `mixed/averaged`, rather than forcing these into in-plane or through-plane.
- Measured quantity: LFA, ISO 22007-3 temperature wave, TDTR and lock-in IR are **diffusivity-primary** (k needs ρ·Cp). TPS and THB fit k and α together. Hot wire, QTM, D5470, E1530 and HFM are **conductivity/resistance-primary**. MTPS is **effusivity-primary** (√(kρCp)), so k needs calibration or ρCp.
- Chinese TPS clones (e.g. Xiangyi DRE-series "快速导热系数测试仪", Hesheng HS-DR-5) should be classed as `TPS_isotropic`. Their identity could not be confirmed (see Gaps).

## Q2. LFA: what k = α·ρ·Cp implies (DSC vs assumed Cp; measured vs rule-of-mixtures ρ) and which data-reduction models matter for thin polymer-composite discs

### Takeaway
LFA gives only α, so k inherits the errors of Cp and ρ. Cp is often the largest term: it is ±5% when measured comparatively in the LFA (vendor) and roughly 1.5–4% by careful ASTM E1269 DSC. For thin, low-diffusivity, semi-transparent BN/epoxy discs the critical choices are the coating (graphite or gold), the heat-loss and finite-pulse model (Cape-Lehman is generally preferred over Cowan) and, for translucent samples, radiative/diathermic models.

### Cited Findings
- k = α·ρ·Cp. ρ and Cp "should be known or determined from separate experiments, which may result in the 'stack up' of uncertainties" — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469)
- Worked example of stack-up: an LFA study of Al/oxide and Al/Teflon composites estimated 7.66% uncertainty for α, 18.23% for heat capacity and 22.7% for k. Cp dominates the k uncertainty — [TTU thesis, ETD-TTU-2011-05-1406](https://tdl-ir.tdl.org/handle/2346/ETD-TTU-2011-05-1406)
- NETZSCH LFA 467 (vendor): α accuracy ±3% against Cp (comparative LFA method) accuracy ±5%. Footnoted best-case values are ±1.5% for α (Cu, Pyrex) and ±4% for Cp — [NETZSCH LFA 467 spec](https://analyzing-testing.netzsch.com/_Resources/Persistent/f/9/0/2/f902a7aebecc31191c0f0d9d2ba6d9cb9f9d0e7b/Technical_Specifications_en_LFA_467_Hyperflash_LFA_467_HT_Hyperflash.pdf)
- DSC Cp by ASTM E1269 (three-scan sapphire method; −100 to 600 °C): a combined uncertainty of 1.7% (0.012 J/g·K) was reported at 600 °C, rising to 5.4% at 1000 °C (Netzsch DSC 404, thesis) — [Univ. Leoben thesis record](https://pureadmin.unileoben.ac.at/portal/en/publications/ermittlung-der-spezifischen-warmekapazitat-mittels-htdsc-von-hoeher-legierten-stahlen(a7a1a6b9-dc62-4314-9fd6-823c7de00c27).html). Sapphire checked under E1269-2011 deviated by 1.3–3.7% — [Physical Testing & Chemical Analysis comparison](https://lhjywlfc.mat-test.com/en/article/id/7d3310bc-22ed-4779-be0a-baf08d1040d8)
- IMEKO paper: a GUM-style uncertainty budget for flash diffusivity, validated on Pyroceram 9606 — [IMEKO proceedings](https://imeko.org/proceedings/calculated-uncertainty-of-the-thermal-diffusivity-measurement-based-on-flash-laser-method)
- Models: Cape-Lehman accounts for finite pulse time, surface radiation and convective loss, which is why it "is generally adopted". It does not model conduction to the holder. On Inconel-600, Parker and Cape-Lehman agreed while Cowan and the other models gave lower values — [KNS 2009 paper](https://kns.org/files/pre_paper/9/269박대규.pdf). Half-rise analysis remains standard practice under ideal conditions (ASTM E1461-13) — [arXiv 1809.06934](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.06934v1). Cowan reportedly fits better at higher temperatures than at lower — [JoVE overview](https://www.jove.com/cn/v/10488/laser-flash-method-for-measuring-thermal-diffusivity)
- Coatings and semi-transparency: graphite restricts absorption depth in semi-transparent samples. The "diathermic" model (Blumm et al.; Mehling et al.) assumes a sample transparent to thermal radiation with grey absorbers on both faces. For NIR-transparent samples, heat moves by radiation as well as conduction, and coupled radiative–conductive models give "substantially different" results — [Luneva et al., arXiv 2008.04079](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2008.04079). COMSOL modelling of graphite-coated thin samples found deviations up to 60% for low-conductivity materials. Below 10 µm thickness, pulse duration rather than coating thickness is decisive — [Semiconductors 2022 (Ioffe)](https://j.ioffe.ru/articles/viewPDF/53700). For about 20 µm polymer films NETZSCH recommends gold over graphite (vendor) — [NETZSCH note](https://analyzing-testing.netzsch.com/pl/application-literature/thermal-diffusivity-of-extremely-thin-polymer-films)
- An analytical finite-element study estimated LFA errors on anisotropic specimens caused by the laser-pulse intensity distribution — [J. Soc. Mater. Sci. Japan 49 (2000)](https://jsms.jp/kaishi/49/paper49-10-15.htm)
- For composites, LFA assumes a homogeneous medium. The standard warns of errors when components have very different diffusivities — [IPACK 2007 paper](https://www.sensorprod.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/wp_tcc-2007-07.pdf). A BSI standards-development draft for circuit-board materials reportedly states that for polymer-composite boards steady state is "the only way to evaluate the true value" and that LFA gives an "apparent value" (from a search snippet; page not loaded) — [BSI project 9024-10087](https://standardsdevelopment.bsigroup.com/projects/9024-10087)
- LFA was found valid and precise against TPS and a guarded heat-flow meter for industrial rubber compounds — [AIP Conf. Proc. 2065, 030041 (2019)](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/acp/article/2065/1/030041/837934/Thermal-conductivity-measurement-of-industrial)

### Inferences
- Root-sum-square of the vendor LFA 467 figures (α ±3%, Cp ±5%, ρ about ±1%) gives about ±6% on k. With DSC Cp at about ±2–3%, k is about ±4–5%. An assumed (handbook or rule-of-mixtures) Cp adds unknown bias. Cp of a two-phase composite follows the mass-fraction rule of mixtures (thermodynamically additive, absent reactions), so assumed Cp is a reasonable approximation *if* the component Cp values and the actual filler mass fraction (e.g. by TGA) are correct.
- Density by rule of mixtures uses the theoretical density. At high BN loadings, porosity makes the true ρ lower, so a rule-of-mixtures ρ inflates k by about the void fraction. Measured α of a porous sample is also lower. For the dataset this suggests a flag: `rho_source ∈ {measured (Archimedes/pycnometer), rule-of-mixtures, unknown}` and `cp_source ∈ {DSC, LFA-comparative, literature/ROM, unknown}`.
- For about 0.5–2 mm BN/epoxy discs (α typically ~0.2–2 mm²/s) t½ is long (~0.1–1 s), so the radiation/convection heat-loss correction matters. Cape-Lehman (or Cowan) plus a pulse correction should be used, and a "Parker-only" analysis is a quality flag. Graphite coating is fine at ≥0.3 mm but problematic for films of tens of µm.
- An LFA k on a pressed/cast disc with in-plane-aligned BN platelets is a *through-plane* value. Labelling it as "k" without direction is the main reason in-plane and through-plane values get mixed in literature compilations.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed worked uncertainty budget specific to BN/epoxy LFA (α + DSC Cp + ρ) was found.
- No quantitative study was found of rule-of-mixtures ρ against measured ρ errors in BN/epoxy at high loading.
- Explicit Cowan and Cape-Lehman equations were not retrieved (the ASTM E1461 text is paywalled).

## Q3. Systematic, method-dependent biases for anisotropic platelet-filled composites (and quantified same-sample method comparisons)

### Takeaway
Three biases dominate. **Isotropic TPS/THB gives √(k_in·k_through)**, which is higher than the through-plane LFA or D5470 value whenever platelets lie in-plane. **LFA on thin discs gives through-plane k**, the low axis for in-plane-aligned BN. **Steady-state D5470/heat-flow rigs give an *apparent* through-plane k** that depends on contact resistance, pressure and bond line. Documented same-material differences run from about 6% (TPS on nominally isotropic PMMA) and 15–20% (TPS vs LFA, isotropic fillers) to 50% (platelet fillers) and more than 2× (D5470 vs TPS on filled TIMs).

### Cited Findings
- **Geometric-mean mixing in TPS:** the isotropic Hot Disk module returns the "geometrical average of the in-plane and through-plane" k (vendor) — [Hot Disk](https://www.hotdiskinstruments.com/products/measurement-modules/isotropic-standard-module/). Peer-reviewed confirmation is in [RSI 94, 074902](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/rsi/article/94/7/074902/2901368/Thermal-conductivity-vs-depth-profiling-using-the). TDTR at low frequency with a small spot likewise measures √(k_r·k_z) — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469). The 3ω slope method yields the determinant of the k tensor for an arbitrarily oriented anisotropic sample — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469)
- **TPS vs LFA on the same polymer/BN-type compounds (patent):** Hot Disk on 4 mm injection-moulded plates reads about 15–20% higher than LFA on 2 mm plates for isotropic fillers, and up to 50% higher for platelet fillers. Through-plane k on a 4 mm bar can be up to 50% higher than on 2 mm plates (skin/core orientation). Platelets align parallel to the mould surface, and k through the platelet is low compared with in-plane. Results "can only be directly compared if" processing, sampling and measurement are identical — [US10328620B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10328620B2/en) (patent, not peer-reviewed)
- **Interlaboratory PMMA comparison (Rides, Morikawa, Halldahl, Hay, Lobo, Dawson, Allen; *Polym. Test.* 28 (2009) 480–489):** temperature-wave analysis, LFA, TPS, transient line-source and heat-flux meter were used on two PMMAs. Conductivity results and diffusivity results each agreed within about ±10% — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/229371097_Intercomparison_of_thermal_conductivity_and_thermal_diffusivity_methods_for_plastics). TPS "repeatedly yielded 6% higher results than each other method", which the summary attributes to TPS sensing in-plane and through-plane together, so any anisotropy raises it — [Thermtest summary](https://thermtest.com/papers/intercomparison-of-thermal-conductivity-and-thermal-diffusivity-methods-for-plastics)
- **TPS vs ASTM D5470 on 10 commercial filled TIMs (Wang/ORNL, Ihms & Brandenburg/Delphi, Salvador/GM):** TPS repeatability was +0.18% to +1.58%. Vacuum grease, the only filler-free material, agreed well. Laird T-putty values (makers' data by TPS) agreed within +0.49%, −14% and −7%. The materials rated by D5470 "showed much higher thermal conductivity", and TennMax was more than double the TPS value (>50% discrepancy). The explanation is that in thin bond lines (gap ≈ filler size) the TIM becomes anisotropic with k_thru > k_in, and fillers bridge the gap. D5470 apparent k "is not a true material property, but rather, a material characteristic". Recommended practice is TPS for ranking and D5470 for impedance and apparent k under application conditions — [OSTI 1570138](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1570138)
- **LFA vs D5470 for TIMs:** LFA gives bulk k only and cannot capture interface resistance, which D5470 includes. LFA also depends on ρ and Cp — [QATS](https://www.qats.com/cms/2012/06/19/testing-thermal-interface-materials/). Contact resistance between adhesive and substrate can be at least as significant as bulk conduction — [IPACK 2007](https://www.sensorprod.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/wp_tcc-2007-07.pdf)
- **D5470 alignment and reproducibility:** industry reports scatter on identical samples far above the predicted uncertainty. A 1% misalignment often goes undetected. A stepped-bar variant reached ±6.4% (95%) on a gap pad over 18 reloads — [Electronics Cooling 2014](https://www.electronics-cooling.com/2014/02/towards-reproducible-astm-d5470-measurements-lower-cost/)
- **Contact resistance in TPS:** it is the main error source for thin films. A multi-thickness approach on polyolefin films gave up to 26% error, reduced to about 4% with a liquid interface — [Chalmers](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/547882/file/547882_Fulltext.pdf). For low-k materials the pristine-sensor overestimate is about 40% — [LBNL arXiv 1910.01798](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.01798). Through-plane pressure, roughness and similar factors strongly affect contact resistance in layered samples (vendor-adjacent) — [Barnett Technical / Hot Disk battery note](https://barnett-technical.com/steves-solutions/hot-disk-battery-anisotropy/)
- **Magnitude of anisotropy in BN-polymers** (which sets the size of the bias): injection-moulded PVDF/BNNS gave k_in 1.26 against k_through 0.40 W/m·K, while compression-moulded samples were about 0.41 W/m·K and isotropic — [PMC12845601](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12845601/). Hot-pressed PFA/BNNS at 30 wt% gave 4.65 in-plane against 1.94 W/m·K through-plane — [PMC6835865](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6835865/)
- Other filled-TIM evidence: a TPS-vs-D5470 study found one filler-heavy material differing by more than 50%, attributed partly to irregular filler shape — [OSTI 1570138](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1570138)
- A numerical study finds TPS can return in-plane and through-plane k precisely if sensor thickness is neglected. When the method's assumptions are not met, anisotropic results deviate — [Thermtest paper record](https://thermtest.com/papers/theoretical-accuracy-of-anisotropic-thermal-conductivity-determined-by-transient-plane-source-method)

### Inferences
- **Quantitative correction rule for the dataset:** for a TPS-isotropic or THB value k_TPS on a sample with anisotropy ratio A = k_in/k_through, k_through = k_TPS/√A and k_in = k_TPS·√A. For A = 3 (PVDF/BNNS-like), k_TPS ≈ 1.73× the LFA through-plane value. That is consistent in direction and magnitude with the patent's "up to 50% higher". For hot-pressed or vacuum-filtered BNNS films with A = 5–20, isotropic-TPS values can overstate through-plane k by 2.2–4.5×. Without A, these rows should be flagged as `mixed/averaged`, not converted.
- Expected ordering for the same in-plane-aligned BN/epoxy: k_in (in-plane LFA or TPS anisotropic) > k_TPS-isotropic ≈ k_THB ≈ k_hot-wire > k_through (LFA) ≈ intrinsic D5470 slope. Single-thickness D5470 apparent k ≤ intrinsic k_through, because contact resistance is lumped in, unless the bond line is thin enough for filler bridging, which can push it above (ORNL case).
- Samples for TPS must be thicker than the probing depth. Thin (≤1–2 mm) cast discs measured with an ordinary sensor in isotropic mode risk boundary reflections, an additional upward or downward bias that depends on the backing. This should be checked against the reported sensor radius and sample thickness.
- An analysis flag set for the dataset: `bias_flag_mixed_direction` (TPS-iso, THB, hot-wire, MTPS), `bias_flag_contact_included` (single-thickness D5470/DRL/E1530), `bias_flag_assumed_cp_rho` (LFA without DSC or measured ρ), `bias_flag_thin_TPS` (sample thickness < sensor diameter, no slab or thin-film module).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed head-to-head study was found with BN/epoxy samples measured by LFA, TPS (isotropic and anisotropic) and D5470 on the *same* specimen. The 15–20% / 50% figures come from a patent.
- Search snippets (ResearchGate) also mentioned "up to 20% difference in diffusivity" and "factor of two" discrepancies. It could not be confirmed which paper these came from (Rides 2009 or a CuCrZr/PMMA intercomparison) or which methods were involved, so they are excluded from findings.
- The NIST–NPL bilateral guarded-hot-plate comparison (NIST TN 2059) exists but its numbers were not retrieved — [NIST TN 2059](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/TechnicalNotes/NIST.TN.2059.pdf). A PTB-specific polymer comparison was not found. An ISO technical report on interlaboratory PMMA testing exists but its content was not accessed — [BSI listing](https://shop-checkout.bsigroup.com/products/plastics-determination-of-thermal-conductivity-and-thermal-diffusivity-results-of-interlaboratory-testing-of-polymethyl-methacrylate-samples)
- No formal D5470 round robin (precision and bias statement) was found. Electronics Cooling notes that one "is still required".

## Q5. Methods recommended/standard in semiconductor packaging and TIM practice (JEDEC JESD51, ASTM D5470) and why

### Takeaway
For TIMs and insulating pads, ASTM D5470 is the industry-standard bulk/interface test because it measures thermal impedance under controlled pressure and bond line, which is the quantity package designers need. JEDEC's JESD51 series characterises *packages* (e.g. JESD51-14 transient dual-interface R_θJC), not TIM bulk k. Transient bulk methods (LFA, TPS) are used for material ranking and R&D.

### Cited Findings
- JESD51-14 (Nov 2010, committee JC-15), the "Transient Dual Interface Measurement", measures junction-to-case R_θJC for single-path heat flow. It takes two transients that differ only in whether a TIM is applied on the case face, and the difference reflects the change in interface resistance. It was validated by a round robin across five JC-15 member labs and gives "much higher accuracy and repeatability than classical steady-state measurements" — [JEDEC JESD51-14](https://www.jedec.org/standards-documents/docs/jesd51-14-0); [Electronics Cooling 2011](https://www.electronics-cooling.com/2011/04/new-thermal-testing-standard/). Its applicability beyond 1-D single-path packages was questioned in 2024 — [Electronics Cooling 2024](https://www.electronics-cooling.com/2024/04/the-applicability-of-jesd51-14-for-the-determination-of-junction-to-case-thermal-resistance/)
- ASTM D5470 is "the only standard method to determine thermal contact resistance". Apparent k can be derived as a function of spacing. The ORNL authors recommend TPS for ranking and D5470 for impedance under application conditions — [OSTI 1570138](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1570138)
- The 1-D steady-state method is "widely used for characterizing the thermal conductivity of TIMs, as well as the thermal contact resistance" (ASTM D5470) — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469)
- D5470 targets "thermally conductive electrical insulation materials", from liquids to hard solids, and reports thermal impedance plus apparent k — [ASTM D5470-17(2024) listing](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/astm/astmd5470172024); [search summary](https://peer.asee.org/an-independent-study-on-designing-and-building-of-an-astm-d5470-standard-apparatus-for-testing-thermal-performance-of-various-materials.pdf)
- Commercial TIM datasheets list D5470 as the test method for most products (e.g. Honeywell PTM, TennMax, Parker Gel30 in the ORNL table) — [OSTI 1570138](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1570138)
- E1530 guarded heat-flow meter is described as "particularly useful for homogeneous, multilayer, and composite specimens" (0.1–30 W/m·K) — [E1530-19 listing](https://eshop.normservis.sk/norma/astm-e1530-19-1.2.2019.html)

### Inferences
- For the course write-up: D5470 is favoured in packaging because the figure of merit for a TIM is R_th = BLT/k + R_c1 + R_c2 at the application pressure. LFA and TPS report only the bulk k term. Literature k values for BN/epoxy measured by LFA are therefore upper bounds on in-package performance unless BLT and R_c are small.
- JESD51 numbers (R_θJC, R_θJA) are package-level and should not be compared with material k rows. If any of the 95 papers report them, they belong in a separate field.

### Gaps
- No JEDEC document defining a TIM bulk-k method was found (and none is believed to exist). The JESD51-1/-2/-12 general scopes were not retrieved.
- No source quantified the share of the TIM industry using D5470 against LFA or TPS.

## Q6. Reference materials used for calibration/validation

### Takeaway
The best-documented certified references for flash and k methods near room temperature are **Pyroceram 9606 (IRMM BCR-724; k and α, RT–1025 K)** and **Pyrex 7740 (BCR-039; k only, −75 to 195 °C)**. Polymer-range checks often use PMMA (used in interlab studies) and instrument-maker reference plates (KEM QTM, Hot Disk). No current NIST thermal-diffusivity SRM exists.

### Cited Findings
- Pyroceram 9606 has been distributed by IRMM since May 2007 as BCR-724, certified for k and α from RT to 1025 K. Values came from an interlaboratory exercise, with α by laser-flash and xenon-lamp methods — [JRC publication JRC30964](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC30964)
- Pyrex 7740 is certified by BCR for k only (CRM 039, −75 °C to 195 °C). Its α and Cp are not certified. There is no recent NIST SRM for diffusivity, and SRM certificates typically expire in 5 years and apply to a specific lot — [ORNL: Reference materials for laser and xenon flash systems](https://www.ornl.gov/publication/reference-materials-laser-and-xenon-flash-thermal-diffusivity-systems); [Thermtest paper record](https://thermtest.com/papers/reference-materials-for-thermal-transport-properties)
- Widely used references include Pyrex 7740, Pyroceram 9606, 304 stainless steel and Armco iron. IG-110 graphite has been used as a pilot LFA reference — [search summary of the same sources](https://www.ornl.gov/publication/reference-materials-laser-and-xenon-flash-thermal-diffusivity-systems)
- The LFA uncertainty budget was validated on Pyroceram 9606 — [IMEKO](https://imeko.org/proceedings/calculated-uncertainty-of-the-thermal-diffusivity-measurement-based-on-flash-laser-method). NETZSCH LFA 467 best-case accuracy was demonstrated on Cu and Pyrex (vendor) — [NETZSCH spec](https://analyzing-testing.netzsch.com/_Resources/Persistent/f/9/0/2/f902a7aebecc31191c0f0d9d2ba6d9cb9f9d0e7b/Technical_Specifications_en_LFA_467_Hyperflash_LFA_467_HT_Hyperflash.pdf)
- PMMA served as the common material in the 2009 five-method plastics intercomparison — [Rides et al.](https://thermtest.com/papers/intercomparison-of-thermal-conductivity-and-thermal-diffusivity-methods-for-plastics). A separate PMMA GHP/GHF intercomparison from −70 to +80 °C exists — [search result, ResearchGate CuCrZr/PMMA intercomparison](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259903511_Intercomparison_of_thermal_diffusivity_measurements_on_CuCrZr_and_PMMA)
- KEM QTM is checked against the supplied reference plates (±5% acceptance) — [KEM FAQ](https://www.kem.kyoto/en/faq/heat/902). Users of Chinese heat-flow testers are advised to calibrate repeatedly against several standard plates (anecdotal) — [drymt forum](https://www.drymt.com/threads/34318/?amp=1)
- For E1530 / D5470 heat-flow meters the comparative calibration uses specimens of known k. The cut-bar method is most accurate when k_ref ≈ k_sample — [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08469)

### Inferences
- For BN/epoxy (k ≈ 0.2–10 W/m·K), Pyroceram 9606 (≈4 W/m·K) and Pyrex 7740 (≈1.1 W/m·K) bracket the range well. PMMA (≈0.19 W/m·K) anchors the neat-epoxy end. Papers that report a reference check (Pyroceram or Pyrex for LFA, PMMA for TPS) could be tagged `calibration_reported = true` as a data-quality indicator.

### Gaps
- No certified PMMA or Vespel (SP-1) thermal reference material was confirmed in the sources reached. Vespel is commonly cited anecdotally as a TPS/LFA check standard, but this is unsourced here.
- NIST SRM 1450-series (fibrous-glass board, GHP) values were not retrieved. Those SRMs are for insulation-range k, below the composite range.
